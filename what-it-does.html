Running today, not a roadmap

Everything here is in daily use inside a working construction services company.

A dozen people log in every day to price work, write proposals, get scopes to the crew, and see where jobs stand. Pick the parts that fit your business. You are not buying all of it.

Estimating

Takeoff in, priced spreadsheet out

Drop in the takeoff and get a priced workbook back. Roofing, siding, gutter and painting so far. Your rates and markups sit in one place the owner controls, so a newer estimator can’t quietly price a job wrong.

  • takeoff parsing
  • your unit prices
  • markup rules
  • Excel out
Crew scopes — the one people don’t expect

Upload the proposal, get the scope in English and Spanish

Drop in the signed proposal and it writes the work scope for the crew, in both languages, side by side. Nobody retypes it. Nobody translates it in the parking lot. The people doing the work read it in the language they think in.

Worth knowing

When the hub is yours, it counts what you decide matters

Software you buy off the shelf measures what the software company thought was important. Build your own and it tracks the handful of things you actually manage your business by.

Most companies only ever see the numbers that show up too late — revenue, margin, the year-end. Those tell you what already happened. The useful ones are the few things that happen first every week and tell you what’s coming. Because your people are already doing the work inside the hub, those numbers come out of the normal day. Nobody has to keep a separate spreadsheet to produce them.

  • Quotes out per person, per week
  • Days from site visit to quote sent
  • Open quotes with no follow-up in ten days
  • Work found on a visit that actually got quoted
  • Estimated cost against what the job really cost
  • Training finished before a new hire is on their own

None of this is a chatbot. Your team never sees a prompt and never has to learn anything about AI. They fill in a form and get a document back that your client would recognize.

What becomes possible

The building is the cheap part now. The question is what your business actually needs.

Each of these has been built at least once and works. None of it is sitting on a shelf waiting for you — it would be built around your process, the same as everything else. It’s listed so you know what to ask for. Most people don’t ask for what they don’t know is possible.

Knowledge

Ask the company a question

Answers pulled only from your own approved material, so nobody gets a confident made-up answer. Someone senior approves what goes in. Questions it couldn’t answer get logged, and that list becomes your list of procedures to write — put together by your own team without you having to ask them.

Practice

Roleplay before the real call

A new salesperson practices against a realistic customer and gets scored on how it went, before they are in front of a real one. Their manager can see how they did.

Compliance

Expiry dates that chase themselves

Insurance certificates and licenses read straight off the document, dates tracked, and a reminder before one runs out. Instead of finding out when somebody is already on site.

Follow-up

The nudge that doesn’t depend on remembering

Scheduled reminders and ready-made emails, so a sent quote doesn’t sit for three weeks because the person who sent it got pulled onto a job. It stops on its own once the work is won.

Repeat work

Inspections that turn into quotes

Every inspection or service visit turns up work that should be quoted and usually isn’t. The findings become a priced quote the same day, instead of a note somebody meant to get back to.

Visibility

Summaries, and whether anyone is using it

A daily summary of what moved and a weekly one for leadership. Plus an honest view for the owner of who is actually using the thing. Software nobody opens is worse than no software, and you should find that out in week two, not year two.
