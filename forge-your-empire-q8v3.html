[ Rules ]

The goal

Take every territory on the map, or be the last empire standing.

Ideology

Every empire follows one ideology: Populist, Militarist, Socialist or Realist. Every territory leans toward one of the ideologies in play. Switch the map to View: Ideology to see which way each one leans.

  • A territory that shares your ideology needs only 1 army to hold.
  • One that doesn't needs 3. Its number shows inverted on the map when it's below that.
  • Revolts are checked when each empire ends its turn. Every territory it holds with too few armies may rise up: 35% with 1 army, 25% with 2. It goes over to the empire it believes in, with 5 fresh armies.
  • When you attack a territory that shares your ideology, tied dice go to you. Its people are on your side.
  • Loyal lands pay. Every three territories you hold that share your ideology add one army to your income.
  • Winning hearts. Hold a foreign territory at its full garrison of 3 for 3 turns in a row and it may come round: a 20% chance at the end of each of your turns after that. Once it does, it leans your way for good (until someone else wins it over), needs only 1 army, and counts as loyal. Let the garrison slip and the count starts over.

Opening

Before the first turn, every empire places 9 extra armies, 3 at a time, taking turns. Watch where your rivals build up before you commit the rest.

Each turn

  • Deploy. You get one army for every three territories (never fewer than three), one more for every three loyal territories, plus a bonus for each whole continent you hold. Click your territories to place them. Shift-click places five.
  • Attack. Click one of your territories with two or more armies, then a neighbour. Lines across the water are sea routes and count as neighbours. Roll once, or Blitz to fight it out.
  • Redeploy. One move of armies between two of your neighbouring territories. Then your turn ends.

The leader

When one empire holds more than a third of the map and is well ahead of the next, the others stop fighting each other and turn on it. The Empires panel marks it as the target. That includes you. An empire down to its last three territories is still fair game for anyone.

Difficulty

Easy: the computer takes bad fights, wanders, and sometimes forgets its garrisons. Normal: it plays straight. Hard: it picks its fights, plans for continents, guards the borders you threaten, turns on you sooner when you lead, and gets 1 extra army a turn.

Battles

The attacker rolls up to three dice (one fewer than the armies in the attacking territory), the defender up to two. Highest dice are matched; each loss removes one army. Ties go to the defender, except under the ideology rule above.
